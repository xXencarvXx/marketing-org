## 5. Why one person should not own both the website and the CRM

**Claim A. The CRM owner is a distinct, dedicated role, not a task you bolt onto a channel manager.** (high confidence)
Across CRM-role guides, the CRM has its own operational owner (the admin who configures the system, manages access, and maintains data quality) plus a strategic owner (who sets the roadmap and is accountable for commercial value). In larger organizations this is a dedicated position, not a shared side-duty. Sources: [TechnologyAdvice](https://technologyadvice.com/blog/sales/crm-roles-and-responsibilities/), [Vtiger](https://www.vtiger.com/blog/crm-roles-and-responsibilities/), [BLND](https://blnd.agency/crm-agency-roles-admin-vs-manager/).

**Claim B. Governance requires one clear owner per system, and the operator of a channel should not also own the system that measures it.** (high confidence)
Separation of duties exists precisely to reduce conflict of interest and protect data integrity: whoever implements a control should not also approve or audit it. In CRM data governance specifically, every data asset needs one accountable owner, and "without clear ownership, accountability disappears and data quality degrades." The website is a lead-generation channel; the CRM is the system of record that scores and attributes that channel. Putting both under one person means the channel owner also owns their own scorecard. Sources: [Imperva on SoD](https://www.imperva.com/learn/data-security/separation-of-duties/), [LogicManager](https://www.logicmanager.com/resources/corporate-governance/governance-101-why-separation-of-duties-is-non-negotiable/), [SyncMatters CRM data governance](https://syncmatters.com/blog/crm-data-governance-best-practices-for-scaling-teams).

**Claim C. Website management and Marketing Operations are different disciplines with different skills and different scorecards.** (high confidence)
Digital/website roles are customer-facing: content, SEO, UX, measured on traffic, conversion, and engagement. MarOps roles are infrastructural: martech stack, automation, data quality and integrity, measured on process efficiency, data accuracy, and system performance. One person spanning both does neither at depth. Sources: [FullEnrich role comparison](https://fullenrich.com/jobtitle/Digital-Marketing-Specialist-VS-Marketing-Operations-Specialist), [MarketingOps.com skill sets](https://marketingops.com/the-background-11-skill-sets-of-amazing-marketing-operations-professionals/), [Smart Insights website ops](https://www.smartinsights.com/managing-digital-marketing/personal-career-development/succeed-website-operations-manager/).

**Claim D. Undedicated, "little by little" CRM ownership is a documented data-quality failure mode.** (high confidence)
Data quality degrades the moment a cleanup ends unless there is a dedicated owner and steward; "most teams have data consumers but no data owner and no steward." Poor CRM data quality is estimated to cost 15 to 25% of annual revenue. The healthy model splits a data owner (sets policy) from a data steward (the CRM admin who enforces it), both dedicated. A website manager absorbing the CRM gradually is exactly the part-time ownership that decays. Sources: [RevBlack](https://www.revblack.com/articles/data-quality-nightmares-why-your-crm-is-lying-to-you), [ZielLab RevOps data-quality guide](https://ziellab.com/post/crm-data-quality-b2b-revops-guide), [Validity](https://www.validity.com/blog/data-quality-management/).

**Claim E. "Clear single ownership and governance" is a named MarOps best practice, and diffuse or creeping ownership is a named mistake.** (high confidence)
Every tool needs a designated owner; martech problems trace directly to missing ownership and governance, where accountability diffuses and systems "degrade silently." Executives are told to treat martech governance as a leadership function with a dedicated owner who has budget authority. Sources: [ORM-Tech on missing ownership](https://orm-tech.com/news/20260710-martech-stack-issues-trace-to-missing-ownership-and-governan/), [MarketingOps.com stack audit](https://marketingops.com/marketing-tech-stack-audit-guide-for-marketing-operations/), [CMSWire on workflows over vendors](https://www.cmswire.com/digital-experience/why-martech-consolidation-in-2026-requires-fixing-workflows-not-just-cutting-vendors/).

## Why leaving her on the website is the right move (the positive case)

This is not "take something away from her," it is "give both jobs a real owner."

- **The website deserves a dedicated, focused owner.** Content, SEO, UX, and conversion are a full discipline with their own metrics. Someone who owns only the website goes deeper and is measured cleanly on channel performance.
- **Two clear owners beats one blurred one.** Governance best practice is one accountable owner per system. She owns the channel, you own the system of record and the data layer. Nothing falls between, nothing degrades silently.
- **Measurement stays independent.** She runs a channel that gets graded; the grading system (CRM, attribution, reporting) sits with a separate owner, so the numbers stay trustworthy.
- **Specialization protects data quality.** The evidence is that undedicated CRM ownership decays. Keeping her on the website and putting the CRM and data with a dedicated MarOps owner protects both.

## The honest caveat, and why it favors you here

At small scale, bundling these roles is normal and fine; the CRM often sits in IT or ops as a shared duty. The separation argument gets stronger as scale and stakes grow. Ayming is not small scale: you have a group Salesforce (One Salesforce), a real attribution chain, and cross-country data. Your CRM is shared enterprise infrastructure that feeds sales and group reporting, not a marketing channel. That is the structural point that closes the case: the website is one France marketing channel, the CRM is shared enterprise infrastructure. Letting a channel role absorb enterprise infrastructure inverts the altitude. She should own the channel; the infrastructure needs its own owner at the right level, which is your group-level MarOps case.
